Calendar
When the next reading of each indicator is due, and which ones are already late.

How these dates are worked out

Every date here is expected, not official. For the indicators with a fixed public schedule the release rule is stored directly: initial claims every Thursday, the Fed's H.8 every Friday, ifo around the 25th. For the rest the date is derived from the indicator's own behaviour, taking the newest reading the dashboard holds and adding one period plus the publication lag that source has actually been running at.

Use it to plan when to look, not to trade on. The binding calendars are published by BLS, Eurostat, the Federal Reserve and Destatis.

Decision Log
What you looked at, what you concluded, and what you did about it, with the date it happened.

Why this exists

Under the StaRUG crisis-detection duty and in any later Insolvenzverschleppung argument, the question is rarely whether the data existed. It is whether management looked at it, drew a conclusion, and acted. A chart that was on screen proves nothing after the fact; a dated entry saying what was on screen and what followed is evidence.

Each entry stores the dashboard's headline readings automatically, so the record cannot drift from what was actually shown. Add your own assessment and the action taken.

Where this is stored. Entries live in this browser only. They are never uploaded, never leave the machine, and are not visible to anyone else using the dashboard. Clearing site data deletes them, so export regularly and keep the file with your board papers.

Early Warning
Financing conditions and recession risk. Insolvency is a liquidity event, so these track whether credit is available, not just whether demand is strong.

What this section can and cannot do

These indicators cover the external early-warning layer that German law requires directors to maintain under the StaRUG crisis-detection duty: market, cost and financing conditions. They are not a going-concern prognosis (Fortführungsprognose). That needs company-specific figures no external feed can supply, above all a rolling 13-week liquidity plan, your own order book and cancellation rate, customer payment behaviour, and covenant headroom.

All series here are US-sourced. No free euro-area equivalent exists on FRED, though US credit conditions are a global risk signal. The ECB publishes CISS and its Bank Lending Survey through its own portal if you want the European layer added.

Sentiment vs Activity — Divergence Check i
Four matched pairs. In each, the same subject is measured twice: once by asking people, once by counting what happened
Standard deviations from each series' own ten-year average, so the two sides are comparable. Both are oriented so that higher means stronger
US · EU · DEEarly WarningEurostat + FREDz-score, 10y
Stated opinion — what people said in a survey. Business confidence, loan officers' own judgement, the Philadelphia Fed's new-orders balance. Moves early, and moves on mood as well as fact.
Recorded activity — what was actually counted. Industrial output, orders booked at Census, loans on bank balance sheets. Slower to arrive, but it happened.
Reading the bars — each is standard deviations from that series' own ten-year average, so a longer bar means further from normal, not a bigger number. Both point the same way: right is stronger. When the yellow bar reaches further right than the green one, confidence has run ahead of what firms are doing. When green leads, the recovery is real but not yet believed.
